# TOPIC PITCH: "The Psychology of the Dislike"
## Why Bad Reviews Sometimes Sell More

---

## **TOPIC IN ONE LINE**
Why does negative publicity sometimes increase sales, and how can brands strategically leverage controversy for business advantage?

---

## **WHY THIS TOPIC? (Key Talking Points)**

### 1. **Counter-Intuitive & Surprising**
- Most people think bad reviews = bad sales (wrong!)
- Actually shows nuanced understanding of consumer behavior
- Makes for compelling research findings & presentations
- Catches attention immediately

### 2. **Perfect for MBA Students**
- **Directly applicable to marketing strategy** → Brand reputation management
- **Real business data available** → Amazon reviews, ratings, sales data
- **Multiple industries to study** → E-commerce, hospitality, entertainment
- **Combines psychology + economics** → Why do people buy despite negative reviews?
- **Regulatory angle** → How to manage reviews ethically vs. manipulating them

### 3. **You Can Collect Real Data**
- Analyze Amazon/online retailer data (product reviews vs. sales correlation)
- Survey consumers on their review-reading habits
- Interview e-commerce managers about their review strategy
- Case studies readily available (Netflix controversies, Airbnb drama that helped)

### 4. **Highly Relevant to Current Business**
- E-commerce is booming → Review management is critical
- Crisis management is essential → How to turn negative into positive
- Trust & authenticity are trending → People trust mixed reviews more than all positive
- Social proof paradox → Controversy can increase social media engagement

### 5. **Career Value**
- Brands NEED people who understand crisis management
- Marketing roles increasingly focus on reputation management
- Shows strategic thinking beyond "just get good reviews"
- Highly sought expertise in brand management & PR

## **WHAT YOU CAN SAY IF SHE ASKS:**

### **"Why this instead of simple 'marketing success' topics?"**
> "Because it challenges conventional wisdom. Most businesses assume bad reviews hurt sales, but evidence shows sometimes controversy drives engagement and even increases sales. It shows strategic depth—understanding WHEN and WHY to leverage negative publicity."

### **"Is there enough data to research this?"**
> "Absolutely. E-commerce platforms have millions of reviews with corresponding sales data. We can analyze the correlation between review sentiment and actual purchase behavior. Plus, there are well-documented case studies of brands that benefited from controversy (Netflix's changes, Airbnb safety incidents, etc.)."

### **"How is this different from standard brand management?"**
> "Most research focuses on reputation repair AFTER damage. This investigates the phenomenon of negative publicity DRIVING sales—a counterintuitive insight that challenges traditional marketing wisdom and has real strategic implications."

### **"Who would use this research?"**
> "E-commerce companies deciding how to respond to negative reviews, brands in crisis management situations, marketing teams deciding between damage control vs. leveraging controversy, and PR professionals. It's immediately applicable."

### **"Can you define the scope clearly?"**
> "Yes. Focus on specific industries (e-commerce, hospitality, entertainment), specific types of controversy (quality complaints, safety concerns, ethical issues), and measurable metrics (sales data, engagement, customer retention)."

## **QUICK RESEARCH APPROACH**
- **Phase 1**: Analyze Amazon/e-commerce data → Correlation between review sentiment and sales
- **Phase 2**: Survey consumers → Why they read/trust reviews, perception of controversy
- **Phase 3**: Case studies → 4-5 brands that benefited from negative publicity
- **Phase 4**: Framework → When to engage vs. ignore controversy, ethical guidelines
