## Why Expensive = Better in People's Minds

## **TOPIC IN ONE LINE**
How do luxury brands use prestige bias and psychological pricing to make consumers perceive value regardless of actual product cost?

## **WHAT YOU CAN SAY IF SHE ASKS:**

### **"Why this topic instead of general marketing?"**
> "Because it bridges psychology, economics, and brand strategy. Understanding prestige bias isn't just about luxury—it's about how ANY brand can increase perceived value and command premium pricing. That's worth millions in business impact."

### **"How do you measure 'perceived value'?"**
> "Through multiple methods: consumer surveys on price perception vs. actual cost, price elasticity analysis comparing luxury to non-luxury alternatives, brand equity metrics, and psychological pricing experiments. All measurable."

### **"Isn't this just about advertising?"**
> "No, it goes deeper. It's about how branding, scarcity, heritage, exclusivity, and psychological pricing interact to create perception of value. For example, why a $5000 Hermès bag feels worth it while a $5000 unknown brand bag doesn't."

### **"What makes this unique for MBA research?"**
> "Most luxury research is qualitative. This can combine quantitative price perception analysis with qualitative brand storytelling—showing exactly HOW brands create the 'expensive = better' perception and WHAT ROI it delivers."

### **"Who would use this research?"**
> "Brand managers deciding on premium positioning, pricing teams, e-commerce platforms, new brands wanting to position as luxury, FMCG companies trying to move upmarket, and retailers managing brand perception."
