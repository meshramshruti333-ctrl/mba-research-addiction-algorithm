# TOPIC PITCH: "The Addiction Algorithm"
## Quick Overview for Guide Discussion

---

## **TOPIC IN ONE LINE**
How do social media platforms deliberately design addictive features, and what are the business & ethical implications?

---

## **WHY THIS TOPIC? (Key Talking Points)**

### 1. **It's Trending & Relevant**
- Currently in news (Senate hearings, TikTok controversy, mental health crisis)
- Shows you're aware of real-world business issues
- Regulatory bodies worldwide are investigating this (EU, UK, US)

### 2. **Perfect for MBA Students**
- **NOT too theoretical** → Has real business applications
- **NOT too narrow** → Combines multiple MBA disciplines:
  - Marketing (consumer psychology, ethical engagement)
  - Strategy (competitive analysis, business models)
  - Technology (algorithms, product design)
  - Ethics (corporate responsibility)
- **NOT too broad** → Can be researched in 12-16 weeks
- **NOT too simple** → Shows sophisticated thinking about current issues

### 3. **You Can Actually Collect Real Data**
- Can survey MBA peers about social media habits (sample available!)
- Can interview heavy users about their experience
- Can analyze publicly available platform data
- Doesn't require insider access or proprietary information

### 4. **It's Eye-Catching & Engaging**
- Every MBA student uses social media → Personal relevance
- Controversial angle → Interesting to discuss
- Easy to visualize → Good for presentations
- Real companies involved → Easy case studies (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube)

### 5. **Career Value**
- Growing demand for professionals who understand:
  - Ethical marketing
  - Product management
  - Digital strategy with consumer psychology
- Shows you think beyond profit → Ethical leadership angle

---

## **WHAT YOU CAN SAY IF SHE ASKS:**

### **"Why this specific topic instead of general marketing?"**
> "Because it combines multiple business disciplines and has real ethical implications. It's not just about 'how to sell more' but 'how to engage responsibly.' Companies are actually being regulated on this now, so it's a critical skill for future leaders."

### **"How is this different from other psychology topics?"**
> "It's grounded in actual business metrics and platform algorithms. We can measure engagement, retention, and revenue impact. Plus, it connects to emerging regulations like the Digital Services Act, so it has policy implications."

### **"Can you actually research this in the given time?"**
> "Yes. I can gather data through surveys (MBA students), interviews (social media users), analyze case studies of existing platforms, and review published research. All these sources are accessible."

### **"What makes this unique?"**
> "Most research is either pure psychology OR pure business. This bridges both—understanding the psychology behind why features work AND the business strategy behind using them. It's also timely with regulatory scrutiny."

### **"Who would care about this research?"**
> "Marketing teams (ethical engagement strategies), product managers (designing non-addictive features), companies facing criticism (reputation management), and policymakers (regulation). It's relevant across sectors."

## **QUICK RESEARCH APPROACH**
- **Phase 1**: Survey ~300 MBA students on social media habits & manipulation awareness
- **Phase 2**: Interview 15-20 heavy users about their experience
- **Phase 3**: Deep-dive case studies on 3-4 platforms (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, LinkedIn)
- **Phase 4**: Develop ethical framework & business recommendations
